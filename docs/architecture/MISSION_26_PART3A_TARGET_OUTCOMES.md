# Mission 26 Part 3A — forecast targets and outcome eligibility

Status: architecture candidate. This is a versioned target catalog and evaluation contract, not a mounted forecast, actual-outcome reader, backtest, calibrated probability, or permission to consume a source. Parts 3B–D and the family slices must implement and validate their own readers before a target can produce a number.

## Common outcome boundary

A forecast target names **one observable measure**, unit and version. Its horizon is an exact half-open UTC interval `[startsAt, endsAt)` derived from the pinned local reporting window where applicable. These v1 targets require `startsAt >= asOf`, narrowing the more permissive Part 1B output *shape*. A current-period nowcast whose horizon starts before prediction needs a separate, explicitly versioned known-prefix/future-remainder target and evaluation policy; it cannot pass this catalog's v1 eligibility gate. An as-of snapshot may contain only facts visible at the prediction cutoff. A later outcome read occurs after the horizon closes, through the owning mission's current tenant, purpose, consent, retention and revision authority. It pins a separate outcome cutoff and never rewrites the forecast or its source receipt. A value recorded after the prediction cutoff may be a legitimate *outcome*, but never a historical feature of that prediction. A late correction may supersede an outcome evaluation with a new version; it must not change the saved forecast.

For an **event-flow** target, the qualifying event must occur inside the future-start horizon. Its source must distinguish event occurrence from ingestion/record time and apply one documented attribution and deduplication key. For an **end-stock** target, evaluate the authorized state visible at the horizon end; do not sum daily stocks. For an **as-of cohort transition** target, freeze the eligible identities at prediction time and count only their first qualifying transition inside the horizon **strictly after** `asOf`; a transition at or before the cutoff was already knowable and is never a future success. A later-created lead or appointment cannot enter that denominator. A completed horizon alone does not establish a complete actual: source finality, delayed arrivals, open dispositions, corrections, consent and retention must be checked. There is no generic assumption that an absent row means zero.

The numerator and denominator of a probability target must share tenant, frozen cohort, service/area filters, outcome window and version. A zero eligible denominator produces `unavailable`, not 0%. If an outcome is not yet finalized, its evaluation is pending or unavailable; a future reader must not score it as a miss. A target may use a reporting window whose calendar is unknown only if its definition explicitly requires elapsed time rather than business-open time. Different time zones, service areas, currency, event definitions, partial periods or changed business calendars cannot be pooled without an explicit comparable-window decision from Part 2B and target-specific normalization.

**Coverage buckets are mutually exclusive at the observation grain named by the target.** Each candidate identity or expected period/source partition is included, excluded by a documented non-applicability rule, missing, stale or conflicting exactly once. A single record can cause more than one diagnostic reason, but not multiple coverage counts. A missing entire source partition is not silently represented by an empty set of observations. Source-specific readiness must establish whether a zero is a complete observed zero. Training, backtesting and display use the same target version, observation grain, unit, eligibility, correction policy and filters.

## Target catalog by future family

The identities below use `key.v1` as shorthand for output-contract fields `target.key = key` and `target.definitionVersion = v1`. They are proposed definitions, **not active target registrations**. A family slice must bind each key to an authorized as-of reader and later outcome reader, prove source coverage, and run Part 3B–D evaluation gates before issue. Materially different attribution, follow-up period, unit, currency or denominator requires a new definition version, not an in-place reinterpretation. A service or area filter is applied only when the owning source can establish it; otherwise the filtered target is unavailable rather than guessed.

Some catalog entries intentionally name unavailable source authority. Their presence defines what a later family means; it does **not** make the source ready or promise that every tenant can see a number. In particular, an earned-revenue recognition basis is not established merely by the Mission 27 invoice/payment roadmap; if no existing source authority supplies one, that is an **unowned source-authority gap** for founder review before `revenue.earned_value.v1` can be activated. Part 3A does not pick statistical minimum samples, claim outcome finality, or set an arbitrary universal waiting period. Those thresholds and source-specific lags must be versioned and measured in Parts 3B–C, with an unavailable state when the evidence is weak.

## Eligibility and backtest join

An evaluation candidate must pin: target key/version; tenant; forecast run and prediction cutoff; horizon and reporting-window digest; declared service and area; source-snapshot digest; frozen cohort digest where applicable; unit/currency; algorithm version; later authorized outcome reader and outcome cutoff; source revision/consent/retention status; and a coverage record. The backtest join is on these identities and the target's attribution rule, **never** merely on customer name, a coincident date, or a dollar amount. Paid and fictional demo identities and evidence remain disjoint.

Reject or mark unavailable when: the source was not authorized at the relevant cutoff; outcome evidence is late, missing, stale, conflicting, revoked or legally deleted; a needed relationship is unreviewed; the horizon has not closed; a cohort denominator is zero; the target's unit, currency, area, service or time basis changed without a versioned compatibility rule; a customer scope change makes an estimate-to-actual comparison invalid; or the owning source cannot certify its event and revision semantics. Backtests may later evaluate the surviving cohort, but must disclose exclusions and never select only easy successes. Historical feature snapshots and later actuals remain distinct immutable receipts where retention permits.

Part 3B owns saved-prediction and actual-observation joins and rolling-origin backtests. Part 3C owns sample sufficiency, error, interval coverage, calibration, drift and applicability gates. Part 3D owns algorithm registration and human-reviewed promotion. Parts 4–8 own family-specific source readers, computation and UI; Part 9 owns scenarios and distributions. Part 11 owns immutable run storage and later display invalidation. No source read, forecast, probability or commercial action is mounted by this document.
