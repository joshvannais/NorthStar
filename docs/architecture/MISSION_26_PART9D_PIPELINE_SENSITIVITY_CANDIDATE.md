# Mission 26 Part 9D — bounded pipeline sensitivity prerequisite

The unmounted `pipelineSensitivityCandidate` asks one narrow question about Part 9C's claimed open-pipeline scenarios: if an estimator changes the **base assumed conversion weight for one open estimate**, how does that estimate's category total change? It recomputes through Part 6B's exact integer arithmetic. A reverse query finds the minimum in-bounds weight for that one estimate to meet a stated **minimum category total**, holding every other estimate at its base assumption. If even the selected weight's declared upper bound cannot meet the target, it reports `target_exceeds_selected_weight_bound` and the binding bound instead of inventing an answer. This is not a general optimization engine or an analysis of staffing, equipment, cost or margin constraints.

The original source, estimate price/status, named assumptions, attribution and declared bounds are caller-supplied. The helper does not verify them, calibrate a conversion probability, or issue a revenue forecast. It returns `forecastIssued: false`, `sourceAuthenticated: false` and `probabilityCalibrated: false`. Incomplete claimed source coverage withholds baseline, change and reverse values. The stated target is a user-chosen what-if threshold, not a promise of revenue. Booked work and earned revenue remain excluded.

This is a narrow prerequisite, not final Part 9D acceptance. Later source integration must authenticate the same-tenant estimate revision, complete as-of pipeline, current permission, applicable assumption bounds and provenance before owner-facing sensitivity. A future broader engine needs reviewed target/constraint definitions and must return unavailable for unsupported or infeasible requests. Synthetic tests prove exact forward/reverse calculations, a binding upper bound, incomplete-source refusal and invalid-input refusal; they do not prove real-world conversion behavior.
