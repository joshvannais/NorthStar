# Mission 26 Part 3D — algorithm identity, promotion and rollback

Status: unmounted governance candidate. No forecast algorithm is registered for execution, no current-versus-candidate evaluation is authenticated, and no promotion or rollback route exists. This slice cannot make a saved forecast, provider result or synthetic backtest into a trusted statistical model.

## Versioned registry boundary

An algorithm definition must identify its target key and definition version; algorithm key and immutable version; deterministic or statistical method; reviewed implementation/build digest and parameters; accepted feature definitions; output contract version; supported reporting grains; training/calibration receipt and policy where applicable; and exact tenant/context applicability. Changing the calculation, target meaning, input policy, training data, attribution, unit or interval policy requires a new version, never a silent edit. The code implementation digest is a **claim** until a trusted build/registration workflow proves it. A registered definition is not an activated forecast or a source grant.

`src/forecasting/algorithmCatalog.js` provides a bounded pure **tenant-identified** catalog for candidate descriptors. It validates shape, distinct key/version identities, target and output-version pins, method kind, feature digests and supported grains, then returns a detached sorted digest. It contains no active pointer, trained model, arbitrary confidence threshold, external provider call or runtime calculation. Its tenant ID is a caller-supplied identity, **not authorization**; a future guarded reader must verify ownership of every feature/training receipt against the server-derived tenant. Deterministic algorithms may not claim training evidence. Statistical candidates require a training-receipt digest but remain untrusted until that evidence and tenant isolation are verified. A globally reusable implementation never implies cross-tenant pooling of private observations or calibration.

## Candidate versus current

The comparator in Part 3B evaluates one algorithm version per result; the Part 3C descriptive aggregate does not prove population completeness or matched origins. Therefore two aggregate errors cannot authorize promotion. A future review must compare candidate and current on the **same eligible prediction origins and outcomes**, with historical feature availability pinned at each origin. It must include every missed/unavailable/normalization-required case, matched run IDs and outcome receipt identities, target/context and reporting-window compatibility, algorithm/source/policy versions, an independent held-out period, and separate signed/absolute error, interval coverage where truly calibrated, drift, sufficiency and downside. Forecasts generated after an actual became visible are not valid historical predictions merely because they use an old as-of label.

The target-specific approved evaluation policy must define relevant metrics, sample/coverage thresholds, permissible tradeoffs, source-finality lag and the conditions for unavailable or rollback. There is no universal minimum sample or automatic winner. A candidate that performs better on one metric but worse on material downside remains a human decision. If complete matched evidence, current permissions or policy are absent, the result is `promotion_unavailable` with a reason, not approval.

## Human-reviewed promotion and recoverable rollback

The future active selection is a tenant/context-scoped versioned pointer, separate from algorithm definitions, evaluation receipts and saved forecast runs. A mutation requires a current owner/administrator forecast-specific permission, server-derived tenant, CSRF, idempotency key, exact expected pointer revision, reviewed comparison digest, reason, actor and timestamp. It must recheck source purpose/retention and evaluation currentness at decision time. It cannot inherit generic dashboard or Learning Center permission. A switch affects **future** forecast execution only: old output/run digests, approved estimates, schedules, invoices and historical evaluation records remain unchanged.

Rollback is a new reviewed pointer event to a previously validated definition and compatible source contract, not deletion or alteration of earlier decisions. It needs the same permissions and expected-revision guard, plus a reason and reference to the promotion being reversed. If the old version's inputs or source permission have since become unavailable, the rollback is blocked and an authorized owner must select another supported version or leave the target unavailable. Recovery from a failed deployment must not silently repoint a tenant. Promotions and rollback events belong to the future guarded Mission 26 run/governance store in Part 11; Part 3D establishes their contract and review standard. No algorithm may self-promote, and neither Polaris advice nor an LLM can sign the human decision.

## Security, demo and acceptance

Definitions may be reusable code; training receipts, calibration, business costs and outcomes stay tenant-private by default. A demo uses fictional isolated definitions/evidence or a shared pure implementation against synthetic inputs, never a paid tenant's model parameters or results. A reviewed promotion does not authorize a provider, feature reader, prediction route, customer communication or operational action.

Acceptance of the pure catalog should prove duplicate and malformed definitions fail closed; input mutation does not change a returned digest; ordering is deterministic; statistical evidence is required as a typed pin and deterministic definitions cannot masquerade as trained. Production acceptance later must prove trusted implementation hash, authenticated matched-origin comparison and sample policy, current tenant/role/source permission, cross-tenant denial, CSRF/idempotency/concurrent revision conflicts, no hidden mutation on a dry-run, immutable audit, rollback after changed source permissions, current-result invalidation, paid/demo isolation and preserved historical runs. Until then Part 3D has no active algorithm or human promotion behavior.
