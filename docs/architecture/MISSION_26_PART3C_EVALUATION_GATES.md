# Mission 26 Part 3C — evaluation and calibration gates

Status: unmounted descriptive-measurement candidate. No authenticated saved forecast, authorized actual reader, verified calibrated interval, empirical accuracy, or production evaluation exists. Part 3B's released comparator is an unmounted input contract, not evidence that any target can be forecast. This slice defines what a later measurement must prove before an output may claim confidence.

## Evaluation population and authority

An evaluation pins the Part 3B result digest, tenant, target definition, unit/currency, service/area scope, algorithm version, reporting-window basis, evaluation policy version and the exact included/excluded origin manifest. It never rewrites a saved prediction or owning actual. Recomputed evaluations after corrected, revoked or late actuals become new immutable revisions under Part 11; an old digest must not be silently replaced. A caller-supplied digest is not source authentication. Parts 4–8 must supply authorized target-specific forecast and actual readers, Part 11B must preserve saved runs, and Part 3D must register/review evaluation policies before a production evaluation can be trusted.

The **denominator** is every eligible historical prediction origin presented to the comparator, including forecast unavailable, missing/stale/conflicting/revoked/pending outcome and reporting-window normalization required. A measurement states the paired count, excluded counts by reason, earliest/latest origin and later outcome cutoff. It never silently drops difficult cases to make error look smaller. Multiple saved candidates for one horizon remain distinct; comparisons between candidate and current versions must use a reviewed matched-origin policy, not a best-after-the-fact selection. No cross-tenant, paid/demo, target-version, algorithm-version, currency, service/area, cohort or incomparable-window pooling.

## Numerical rules and unavailable states

Only `paired` Part 3B receipts with a finalized known outcome may enter arithmetic. A known zero remains zero; an absent value is unavailable. Use exact decimal or bounded integer arithmetic and preserve the target's unit. Point forecast error is forecast minus actual; for ranges, the designated central value is the error comparator. Report count, signed bias and absolute error with an explicit aggregation definition. Relative measures such as percentage error are unavailable when their denominator is zero. Do not compare dollar errors to count errors, or sum overlapping forecast targets as though they were independent.

Interval hit rate is a descriptive count of finalized actuals within the saved lower and upper endpoints. A deterministic favorable/base/adverse range is a **scenario envelope**, not a statistical interval. Only a saved `calibrated_interval` with a versioned nominal coverage level and sufficiently independent outcome evidence can support a coverage-versus-nominal calibration test. Output v1 has no nominal coverage field, so an interval-hit fraction alone cannot claim calibration. A model's self-declared `confidence.backtestDigest` is not proof that the underlying evaluation is valid. Until the versioned interval policy, authenticated source lineage, sample sufficiency and out-of-sample checks exist, calibrated probability and confidence are unavailable.

Sample sufficiency is target-, horizon-, context- and decision-specific. A later reviewed policy must specify minimum distinct origins, paired count, coverage fraction, observation lag, applicable temporal span and any independence or concentration rule. This document chooses no universal `N` or accuracy target. An absent policy, weak coverage, too few independent origins, unresolved source correction, no comparable windows or missing nominal interval definition yields a named **unavailable** state, not a passing score or fabricated precision. A descriptive error computed on synthetic fixtures is not a contractor-specific accuracy claim.

Drift compares a frozen reference window against a later nonoverlapping window with the same target, algorithm, units, context and policy. A reviewed policy defines window lengths, lag, meaningful change and action. Distinguish prediction error drift, input/source coverage change, business calendar/area change and legitimate scope or operating-policy change; do not interpret all variance as model degradation. Drift evidence can prompt human review or disable a confidence claim, but cannot automatically promote a new model, edit an estimate, change pricing, staff a crew or dispatch work. Part 3D owns algorithm promotion and rollback.

Applicability is narrower than a global score. The result must identify the tenant and exact target/context on which it was measured, the supported prediction horizons, data recency and excluded conditions. A model evaluated on one business or service area cannot borrow another tenant's private production rates or claim accuracy for an untested area. Mission 25 learning remains tenant-private; Mission 26 can consume only authorized and attributable outcome facts.

## Bounded implementation and acceptance

`src/forecasting/evaluationGates.js` rebuilds the bounded Part 3B comparison from supplied receipts rather than trusting a caller-selected subset or copied digest. It produces exact-unit total/mean absolute error and signed bias for paired outcomes, preserving the full status denominator. A fractional expected count remains valid against an integer observed count. It counts deterministic scenario-envelope hits separately from calibrated-interval observations, but deliberately returns `unavailable` for sample sufficiency, calibration and drift because reviewed policies, nominal levels, trusted sources and reference windows are absent. It cannot issue a confidence verdict from arbitrary caller-supplied thresholds. The deterministic result pins the comparison digest and its origin/exclusion counts. `tests/unit/m26-evaluation-gates.test.js` covers missing and zero outcomes, exact decimal arithmetic, normalization-required exclusion, scenario-versus-interval separation and mixed-algorithm refusal. These fixture numbers do not measure production accuracy. Future production integration must additionally prove current permission and retention, owning-reader versions, repeatable replay, corrected-actual supersession, source deletion/revocation, concurrent writes, tenant isolation and paid/fictional-demo separation.

Acceptance should use blinded completed jobs and held-out rolling origins, plus synthetic cases with observed zero, all missing outcomes, one very large outlier, mixed algorithm versions, changed calendar, short versus long horizons, corrected actuals, deterministic scenario ranges, a nominal-level mismatch, sample concentration and drift caused by source policy rather than performance. Report component-level error and coverage; a correct total produced by offsetting mistakes is not evidence of a good model. CI/provider/private-production and physical-device evidence remain separately unavailable until actually observed.
