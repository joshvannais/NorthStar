# Mission 26 Part 4A — inbound demand source and forecast boundary

Status: implementation in progress. The canonical graph is a useful source of accepted inbound records, but no Mission 26 lead-source snapshot, historical saved forecast, demand algorithm or production forecast route exists. A current dashboard count or Retell service guess is not a calibrated forecast.

The unmounted `src/forecasting/inboundDemandCandidate.js` now demonstrates a narrow arithmetic baseline for `demand.inbound_leads.v1`. It accepts only tenant-wide, unfiltered Retell-only complete prior reporting periods and requires at least three comparable periods. Its count mean preserves fractional expected leads and always reports uncalibrated uncertainty. Missing/revoked coverage, unknown or incomparable calendar context and mismatched exposure return unavailable. It rejects future-recorded observations, duplicate periods, other tenants and purported service/area filters. `tests/unit/m26-inbound-demand-candidate.test.js` covers its pure contract. The supplied source and coverage digests and **lead identity** are not authenticated by this module; raw Retell call counts must never be fed in as lead counts. No route or source reader calls it. It is a candidate proof, not a live forecast, certified Retell coverage or accepted Part 4A.

The next unmounted pure projection `src/forecasting/inboundLeadObservations.js` accepts **already reviewed, distinct lead** first-receipt records and explicitly certified reporting periods, then produces period counts for the candidate. It rejects duplicate lead IDs, cross-tenant records, late/future reviews, overlapping or incompatible periods, and malformed inputs; a later reviewed or revoked identity makes its period unavailable, not zero. Its caller-supplied review/source/coverage digests are *shape-checked but not authenticated*. This deliberately does not map Retell calls into lead identities or grant access to any database record. It cannot certify provider completeness, historical corrections, consent or the existence of the needed source authority. The future guarded reader must prove all of those facts before calling it.

## What the current repository proves

`migrations/004_canonical_persistence_v2.sql` stores tenant-scoped canonical operations, transcripts, communications and opportunities. Transcripts and inbound communications retain nullable `occurred_at`; opportunities retain `created_at` and `service_type`. `src/services/canonicalGraphService.js` writes those in one accepted graph operation. The Retell adapter `src/services/canonicalRetellIngestion.js` supplies an external call ID, optional occurrence time and a heuristic service key; the canonical transcript uniqueness constraint prevents reuse of one external call ID within a tenant. Authenticated manual lead creation in `src/routes/canonicalLeads.js` uses an idempotency key but no reviewed cross-request duplicate merge. Legacy lead and call tables are separate. A source row's `created_at` is not proof it was visible to an earlier forecast, and a caller-provided occurrence time is not NorthStar's record time.

Part 2A's released `migrations/136_canonical_forecast_as_of_snapshots.sql` captures only approved estimate decisions under purpose `forecast_pipeline`. Its manifest shape cannot grant lead-source access by changing a caller field. Part 1C has no forecast permission or route. Part 3A defines `demand.inbound_leads.v1` as distinct accepted inbound lead identities with event occurrence inside the horizon, and `demand.requested_service_leads.v1` as one reviewed service classification per lead. These target definitions are future source requirements, not currently active readers.

## Narrow first source and explicit unavailable cases

Accepted Retell inbound canonical opportunities are a possible **call-level source**, explicitly `retell_only`; they do not establish distinct inbound **lead** identity or total company demand. The external call ID prevents duplicate webhook retries, but the same customer can call again about the same job, and current canonical graph writes one opportunity per call without a reviewed cross-call lead merge. Therefore Part 3A's `demand.inbound_leads.v1` cannot be calculated from raw call IDs or opportunity IDs. A call-level source receipt must show completed operation, one opportunity for the call, occurrence time and NorthStar visibility time, with consent/correction status. It remains a source receipt, not a lead forecast. An authoritative same-tenant lead-identity reconciliation and first qualifying receipt policy are prerequisites to mapping those calls to the target. Null/invalid occurrence, incomplete channel coverage, unresolved duplicate/merge, consent/retention loss or missing source-revision history keeps the affected target unavailable rather than zero. A phone call, a distinct lead, a qualified lead and a booking are different measures.

Manual/API leads, forms, SMS, imported leads and legacy tables need their own event-time, identity/dedupe and source-coverage proof before they can enter a tenant-total target. The current Retell `serviceKey` heuristic is not a reviewed, mutually exclusive classification. Requested-service mix must remain unavailable until the canonical lead authority supplies an explicit reviewed service-classification revision and conflict policy. A customer address alone does not prove a mapped service-area assignment. Until authoritative geocoding/area membership exists, area-filtered demand is unavailable rather than guessed from text.

## Safe Part 4A forecast progression

1. Add a separate, purpose-guarded as-of Retell lead-source capture with current owner/admin session, entitlement, tenant, consent/purpose, CSRF/idempotency, bounded size, immutable manifest and source correction/retention rules. Reuse Part 2A's MVCC visibility and guarded database pattern; never synthesize arbitrary historical snapshots from timestamps. The source receipt is a **Retell-only observation**, not a forecast or generic source registration.
2. From authorized snapshots, derive versioned complete observation windows under Part 2B. Separate zero observed leads with certified complete channel coverage from missing records or provider outage. Call IDs may count call events only; count distinct *reviewed lead identities* for the lead target, using their first qualifying receipt and explicit merge/correction lineage. Keep service and area unknown where unreviewed. The current source must not leak later events into an earlier as-of run.
3. Register a deterministic, explicitly limited baseline that uses only historical observed windows visible at prediction time. Preserve expected-count decimals, window/tenant/source digests, algorithm version, coverage and unquantified uncertainty. Where enough comparable windows, source coverage or reviewed applicability is absent, output `unavailable` with a useful reason. A baseline is not calibrated booking probability or a promise of arrivals.
4. Save runs and compare against later finalized actuals under Parts 3B–C and 11. An attractive retrospective fit or an LLM's confidence percentage is not validation. A future Part 4D paid/demo UI must communicate the source limit and unavailable states in plain language, with isolated fictional demo data.

Part 4A may first release a bounded pure observation/forecast contract while the source reader is built and independently reviewed. It must **not** label that candidate a live forecast. The final Part 4A acceptance requires the guarded source path and a genuinely future-facing saved output, or an explicit documented blocker; later Part 4 slices cannot quietly treat a candidate as accepted production evidence. The candidate does not infer service mix or area membership and cannot certify the existence or completeness of any historical period from caller-supplied digests.
