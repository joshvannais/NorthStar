## Reused authority

`src/forecasting/timeSeriesWindows.js` accepts a Business Profile identity, version, hash and raw profile **only after a future source reader has established their tenant, as-of revision and permission**. Its inputs are not self-authenticating. It reuses the existing IANA time-zone and wall-clock resolver in `public/js/scheduling-time-contract.js` and the existing working-hours/holiday evaluator in `src/scheduling/conflictEvaluator.js`. Part 2B only exports that evaluator's already implemented `hoursForDate` helper; it does not change Mission 22 scheduling decisions.

The projection supports a local day, ISO Monday-start week, calendar month, calendar quarter or calendar year. It requires an aligned local start date and resolves each local midnight to a UTC instant. It rejects a nonexistent or repeated boundary rather than guessing an offset. UTC elapsed minutes and business open minutes are separate. A daylight-saving day may have 23 or 25 elapsed hours without changing its ordinary 9-to-5 business hours. A closed holiday has **zero known** open minutes; missing or ambiguous hours produce **unknown**, never zero. Overnight hours belong to their opening local date, matching the reused scheduling evaluator.

Area scope must be explicit: `tenant_all` means no geographic filter, while `profile_area` requires a configured Business Profile service area and pins its digest. An empty placeholder does not become a real area. A radius or travel-time policy requires a known headquarters origin; the area digest includes that origin so an office move cannot silently reuse an old geographic cohort. The profile hash, calendar digest and area digest remain distinct so future feature readers can identify relevant profile changes without copying the whole private profile into a forecast.

`compareReportingWindows` refuses incomplete or internally inconsistent window records, then checks whether two windows share tenant, grain, time zone, service and area context and have known calendars. It reports when elapsed minutes, open minutes or calendar revisions require normalization. This is a structural check, not authentication of the underlying Business Profile: callers must still authorize and pin that source. **Comparable context is not evidence that the source observations are complete, of the same quality, or statistically comparable.** Parts 2C and 3 must still establish source coverage, target definition, outcome windows, seasonal applicability, partial-period treatment and minimum evidence. A calendar-year window is not a tenant's fiscal year unless future Business Profile authority explicitly supplies one. No future-event record is treated as an actual outcome merely because its date lies inside a reporting window.

The source snapshot from [Part 2A](MISSION_26_PART2A_AS_OF_SNAPSHOTS.md) preserves microsecond cutoff precision; these reporting boundaries use exact local-calendar midnight converted to millisecond UTC instants. A future output adapter must never round the source cutoff down and thereby claim an earlier knowledge boundary. No such adapter is mounted in this slice.

Focused tests cover spring/fall daylight changes, period alignment including a leap year, known versus unknown open minutes, changed service area and absent time-zone authority. Physical device, provider, production paid-tenant and real historical forecast evidence are not provided by this internal projection.
